. The Etymological Roots of the New System
1st Person (anga): This is a perfect reflex of the ancient PTB root *ŋa or *ŋa-y (I/me)
. The specific shape ang or ang-a is highly prominent in the Bodo-Garo branch, serving as the 1st person singular pronoun in languages like Garo (ang-a) and Atong (ang)
.
2nd Person (nang): This derives directly from the universal PTB root *na-ŋ (you/thou)
. It is an incredibly unifying word, appearing almost exactly in this form across multiple branches, including Ao Naga
, Konyak
, Karbi (Mikir)
, Zeme
, and Meitei
.
3rd Person Human (paw): This is rooted in the PTB etymons *pa (3rd person pronoun) and *pʷa (man/father/person)
. In Ao Naga, pa serves as the core 3rd person human pronoun (he/she)
. Restricting paw strictly to humans is a brilliant design choice, as it perfectly mirrors the natural animate/human vs. non-human distinctions found in regional grammars like Ao
.
The Plural Suffix (-tem): You have shifted the pluralizer to -tem, which is the exact, standard plural suffix used in the Ao Naga language
. In Ao, -tem is added to unmarked singular nouns to make them plural (e.g., nisóN "person" becomes nisóN-tem "persons")
.

3. Integration into the SOV Grammar Engine
Because Wesean uses a strictly regular Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) structure with postpositional tags
, your new pronouns plug into the existing sentence formulas flawlessly. They will take the standard case tags (like -na for Actor/Subject, -pu for Object, and -ki for Possession).
Examples in Practice:
English: "I see the dog." Wesean Logic: I [Actor] + Dog [Animal-Prefix-Object] + See [Present]. Wesean: Anga-na sakwi-pu madu.
English: "You (plural) will go to the house." Wesean Logic: You-Plural [Actor] + House [Location] + Go [Future]. Wesean: Nang-tem-na kim-lo wapo.
English: "They (human) ate our food." Wesean Logic: They-Human [Actor] + We [Possessive] + Food [Object] + Eat [Past]. Wesean: Paw-tem-na anga-tem-ki am-pu cato.
