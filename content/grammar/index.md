Wesean Language Specification: A Unified Linguistic Framework for the Wesean Region

# 1. Linguistic Mandate and Purpose

The strategic implementation of a unified "Wesean" language is the foundational pillar of the Vanguards of Wesea initiative. This auxiliary framework is not a mere philological curiosity; it is a high-status standardized language designed to foster regional unity, leadership, and a trans-national identity across the Northeastern Indian corridor. Historically, the region’s "extreme diversity" and "difficulty of access" have forced populations into isolated niches within the Himalayan foothills. While the hills remained fragmented, the Brahmaputra plains became "multilingual meeting grounds" prone to low-level pidginization and the creep of external lingua francas.

Wesean is engineered to bridge the "hills and plains" division, replacing trade-jargon with a professional medium for advocacy and excellence. By establishing a standardized linguistic protocol, we provide a tool for youth activists and regional leaders to articulate a narrative of integrity and innovation. Wesean resists the phonetic and grammatical erosion typical of regional shifts, asserting a "United and Sovereign" cultural future through linguistic synthesis and high-value professional prose.

# 2. Sound System (Phonology)


The phonological philosophy of Wesean synthesizes the phonetic depth of the 60% influence group (Meitei, Boro-Garo, Jingphaw, Mizo, Angami, and Ao). The system prioritizes segments that are both historically grounded and resistant to external phonetic influence.

Consonant and Vowel Inventory

The inventory is anchored in Proto-Tibeto-Burman (PTB) and Proto-Tani (PT) reconstructions:

* Stops and Nasals: Includes /p, t, k, b, d, g/ and the essential velar nasal -ŋ (PTB puŋ).
* Syllable-Finals: Wesean maintains a strict distinction between the glottal stop /ʔ/ (occurring as an onset or a specific final) and the underspecified syllable-final consonant -K, reflecting the structural integrity of the Tani branch (Post 3.4.2).
* Vowels: A six-vowel system (/a, e, i, o, u, ɨ, ə/). The high-central unrounded vowel /ɨ/ is designated as a "regional phoneme of resistance," serving as a phonetic bulwark against Indo-Aryan phonetic creep.

Tonal Philosophy

Wesean utilizes a simplified pitch-accent system to maximize intelligibility. Unlike complex contour systems, it focuses on functional distinctions between high and low pitch. This prevents the "segmental losses and syllable erosion" common in the plains while maintaining a high-status register. This system ensures that the semantic weight of the monosyllabic roots is preserved during rapid communication.

# 4. Morphological Processes (Word Formation)

Wesean prioritizes a "shift to greater synthesis" through prefixation and compounding, reflecting the historical evolution from monosyllabic roots to complex semantic systems.

Prefixation (Semantic Templates)

We adopt the Galo templates for semantic categorization:

* a-: Kinship marker and honorific prefix, maintaining social integrity in formal advocacy.
* ta-: A "Natural Class" prefix applied specifically to fauna, flora, and the environment.
* ja-: Result-oriented derivations.
* pV-: Categorization of physical properties and shapes.

Class-Changing Derivations

Wesean utilizes nominalization for professional prose. For example, the verbal root rɨ- (to do) is transformed into the nominalized form rɨbə (action/movement), providing the necessary lexicon for organizational leadership.

Compounding

A "Root-Root" strategy combines ancient PTB ancestors:

* mja (eye) + si (water) = mjasi (tears).
* kam (jaw) + ta (Natural Class) = təmɟja (jaw/cheek).

# 5. Syntax and Sentence Structure

The canonical pattern is SOV (Subject-Object-Verb), the historical pattern of the region.

The Predicate Complex

The Wesean verb follows a rigid structural formula: Root + Aspect + Particle. This "Predicate Complex" allows a single word to communicate manner, result, and social relation.

Clausal Nominalization

To support high-value professional prose, Wesean employs "Clausal Nominalization." This allows for high-density information flow, embedding entire situational contexts as subjects or objects. This structural complexity distinguishes Wesean from low-status pidgins, enabling nuanced political and historical advocacy.

# 6. Tense and Aspect System

Wesean emphasizes an "Aspect-heavy" system over linear Western time, focusing on the status and result of actions—values resonant with the Vanguards of Wesea.

1. Imperfective/Stative (-dùu / -dóo): Ongoing actions or persistent states.
2. Change-of-State (-dàk): Directly linked to innovation and progress. Example: Wesea vangu-dak (Wesea has become the Vanguard).
3. Perfective/Achievement (-tó / -dáa): Completed goals and results.

This system forces the speaker to distinguish between the "action" (effort) and the "result" (outcome), baking accountability into the grammatical structure of the language.

# 7. Grammatical Rules and Case Marking

Wesean utilizes "Relational Marking" to define grammatical relations without resorting to the passive voice.

* Accusative/Non-agentive (-m): Marks the patient or object.
* Genitive/Ablative (-ke): Marks possession, origin, or source.
* Dative/Beneficiary: Identifies the community or individual benefiting from an action.

Prohibition of Passive Voice: The passive voice is strictly forbidden. This reflects the regional logic of active participation and mandates that the speaker identifies the agent of an action, reinforcing the Vanguard ethos of agency and personal responsibility.

# 9. Cultural Elements and Pragmatics

Wesean is a "hill language" designed for modern elevation. It incorporates "Up/Down/Same-Level" deictics essential for the topography of the region:

* tə: Upriver / North / Upwards.
* bə: Downriver / South / Downwards.
* áa: Same level / East-West.

Social pragmatics are governed by "Clan and Village" semantics. The a- prefix serves as an honorific kinship marker, ensuring that formal advocacy remains grounded in ancestral respect and social integrity. This register integrates the Vanguard values of innovation and excellence into everyday interaction.

# 10. Expansion Rules and Standardization

To prevent "pidginization," Wesean strictly regulates the addition of new terms. New concepts are primarily synthesized from existing PTB roots. When specialized terms for ecological or spiritual concepts are required, the language borrows from the 10% tier (specifically Khasi, Karbi, or Tani) to maintain an indigenous character and resist Indo-Aryan creep.

This standardization ensures that Wesean remains a high-status medium capable of supporting a modern regional administration. This document constitutes a Linguistic Covenant for the region—a path toward a unified, self-determined identity that honors the common ancestor Abó Taníi while pioneering the future of the Wesean people.
